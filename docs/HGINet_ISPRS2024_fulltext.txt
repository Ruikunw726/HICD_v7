ABSTRACT  
Current semantic change detection (SCD) methods face challenges in modeling temporal correlations (TCs) 
between bitemporal semantic features and difference features. These methods lead to inaccurate detection re -
sults, particularly for complex SCD scenarios. This paper presents a hierarchical semantic graph interaction 
network (HGINet) for SCD from high-resolution remote sensing images. This multitask neural network combines 
semantic segmentation and change detection tasks. For semantic segmentation, we construct a multilevel 
perceptual aggregation network with a pyramidal architecture. It extracts semantic features that discriminate 
between different categories at multiple levels. We model the correlations between bitemporal semantic features 
using a TC module that enhances the identification of unchanged areas. For change detection, we design a se -
mantic difference interaction module based on a graph convolutional network. It measures the interactions 
among bitemporal semantic features, their corresponding difference features, and the combination of both. 
Extensive experiments on four datasets, namely SECOND, HRSCD, Fuzhou, and Xiamen, show that HGINet 
performs better in identifying changed areas and categories across various scenarios and regions than nine 
existing methods. Compared with the existing methods applied on the four datasets, it achieves the highest F 1
scd 
values of 59.48%, 64.12%, 64.45%, and 84.93%, and SeK values of 19.34%, 14.55%, 18.28%, and 51.12%, 
respectively. Moreover, HGINet mitigates the influence of fake changes caused by seasonal effects, producing 
results with well-delineated boundaries and shapes. Furthermore, HGINet trained on the Fuzhou dataset is 
successfully transferred to the Xiamen dataset, demonstrating its effectiveness and robustness in identifying 
changed areas and categories from high-resolution remote sensing images. The code of our paper is accessible at 
https://github.com/long123524/HGINet-torch .   
1. Introduction 
Change detection plays a crucial role in urban construction, urban 
planning, and environmental monitoring, providing essential informa -
tion on changes in land use and land cover for policy-making ( Lunetta 
et al., 2006; Huang et al., 2017 ). Conventional change detection 
methods mainly depend on time-consuming and labor-intensive field 
surveying and visual interpretation of high-resolution remote sensing 
images. With the advancement of remote sensing technologies, access to 
numerous images has increased, promoting the demand for automati -
cally identifying land changes from these images ( Daudt et al., 2018; 
Zheng et al., 2021; Shu et al., 2022; Mei et al., 2023 ). 
Many studies have focused on binary change detection (BCD) tasks to 
identify changed areas from bitemporal images ( Peng et al., 2019 ; Chen 
et al., 2021a ; Li et al., 2023b . Moreover, semantic change detection 
(SCD) simultaneously detects changed areas and identifies the changed 
categories, providing “ from – to ” change information ( Yang et al., 2021; 
Zhao et al., 2022; Niu et al., 2023 ). Conventional SCD methods primarily 
compare post-classification results of bitemporal images at the pixel 
level to identify changed areas and categories ( Afrasinei et al., 2017; Wu 
et al., 2017; Yan et al., 2019 ). In addition to pixel-based methods, object- 
based methods have been used to detect changes in high-resolution 
images, demonstrating their effectiveness in reducing high-frequency 
pixel noise and extracting diverse object features ( Wang et al., 2018; 
Lv et al., 2020 ; Liu et al., 2021a ). However, object-based methods 
depend on deriving well-segmented objects, which poses challenges in 
complex urban areas where poor segmentation can increase the un -
certainties of detecting changed areas. Nowadays, deep learning based

on convolutional neural networks (CNNs) has gained popularity in 
remote sensing image processing, achieving outstanding performance 
compared to conventional methods owing to its robust feature extrac -
tion capabilities ( Sun et al., 2020 ; Chen et al., 2022a,b ; Shu et al., 2022 ). 
Deep learning methods for SCD generally fall into three architec -
tures: single-branch, dual-branch, and multitask ( Tian et al., 2022 ). 
Typically, single-branch methods directly feed the difference or 
concatenation maps of bitemporal images into a CNN to extract deep 
features regarding changed objects ( Daudt et al., 2018 ). Such methods 
consider each potential change type as an independent class label and 
treat SCD as a straightforward semantic segmentation task ( Ding et al., 
2022 ). The number of changed categories equals the square of the 
number of semantic segmentation classes, causing considerable class 
imbalance. Convolutional dual-branch methods first classify bitemporal 
remote sensing images separately and then identify the changed cate -
gories by comparing the two classification results ( Daudt et al., 2019 ). 
The detection accuracy of these methods highly depends on the separate 
classification results. Furthermore, detection errors may accumulate 
from the two separate classification results, particularly in areas close to 
the object boundaries. Multitask learning architectures, which combine 
semantic segmentation and BCD branches, have led to more advanced 
methods that simultaneously achieve land cover classification and BCD 
( Ding et al., 2022; Xia et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2022; Zheng et al., 2022 ). 
Thus, multiple related tasks, such as semantic segmentation and change 
detection, can be learned together through a feature-sharing scheme 
that enhances feature extraction and increases the accuracy of SCD. 
Recent studies have demonstrated that multitask learning architec -
tures are better suited for SCD than others. They have attracted 
considerable interest in urban SCD studies ( Yang et al., 2021; Tian et al., 
2022 ). Yang et al., (2021) developed an asymmetric Siamese network 
with a triple-branch architecture to separately perform semantic seg -
mentation and BCD tasks. Shen et al., (2022) proposed a feature- 
constrained multitask Siamese network for SCD from high-resolution 
remote sensing images. Xia et al., (2022) constructed a deep Siamese 
post-classification fusion network (PCFN) to decompose SCD into 
bitemporal semantic segmentation and BCD tasks. Thus, semantic rep -
resentations are constrained using change representations during 
training. 
It is challenging to accurately identify the changed categories while 
locating the changed areas, particularly in complex SCD scenarios. Most 
existing studies have used CNN-based feature extractors to obtain dif -
ferences in bitemporal semantic features ( Daudt et al., 2019; Ding et al., 
2022; Xia et al., 2022 ), often overlooking the interactions among 
different feature layers. Due to the locality of convolution operations, 
CNNs are limited in capturing long-distance context information, i.e., 
the surrounding information far from target objects. This limitation 
often results in coarse and imprecise segmentation. Moreover, CNNs are 
inadequate in modeling interactions among different feature layers from 
a global perspective, leading to imprecise detection, particularly in 
complex areas with diverse objects. Graph convolutional networks 
(GCNs) are powerful for learning the relationships among nonstructured 
data. They effectively model global interactions among different feature 
layers through structural learning ( Te et al., 2020 ; Chen et al., 2021c ; 
Wang et al., 2022a ). Bitemporal semantic features derived from un -
changed areas also exhibit high intrasimilarity but low interdissimilarity 
for changed areas. Existing multitask architectures based on triple- 
branch networks ( Daudt et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2021 ) model sub -
tasks that extract semantic features without considering the temporal 
correlations (TCs) among bitemporal semantic features, thus causing 
imprecise predictions for unchanged areas. 
This study aims to develop an effective multitask network for SCD 
from high-resolution remote sensing images. It presents a hierarchical 
semantic graph interaction network (i.e., HGINet) to simultaneously 
improve the identification of changed areas and categories. Initially, an 
effective multilevel feature extractor is designed to enhance the repre -
sentation of bitemporal semantic features. Subsequently, the 
interactions between different feature layers of the extracted features 
and the extracted difference features are quantified by graph learning, 
demonstrating an improvement in the detection ability for complex SCD 
scenarios. Finally, this study focuses on enhancing the correlations 
among bitemporal semantic features for unchanged areas. The contri -
butions of this study are as follows:  
1) It proposes an effective and robust method for detecting semantic 
changes in various settings using high-resolution remote sensing 
images.  
2) It designs a lightweight semantic difference interaction (SDI) module 
using graph learning to characterize the interactions among different 
feature layers, thereby improving the detection capability of HGINet 
for complex SCD scenarios.  
3) It introduces a TC module using a cross-learning method to enhance 
the identification of unchanged areas.  
4) It develops a multilevel perceptual aggregation network (MPANet) 
based on pyramid architecture to improve semantic representations 
of ground objects with various characteristics. 
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 illus -
trates the details of the proposed method, Section 3 describes the SCD 
datasets used, and Section 4 presents the experimental results and 
analysis. Section 5 discusses these findings, followed by the conclusions 
in Section 6 . 
2. Methods 
Fig. 1 shows the workflow of the proposed method (i.e., HGINet) for 
SCD from high-resolution remote sensing images. HGINet employs a 
multitask network architecture to jointly learn category and change 
information through semantic segmentation and change detection tasks. 
This approach constrains the learning of semantic category information 
by locating changed areas, thereby simultaneously improving the 
identification of changed areas and categories. For semantic segmenta -
tion, we present a feature extractor based on an MPANet with a pyra -
midal structure. This extracts and fuses semantic features at different 
levels from bitemporal remote sensing images to obtain a robust se -
mantic representation of ground objects. 
To enhance the identification of unchanged areas, we model the TCs 
of extracted semantic features between bitemporal images through a 
cross-learning method, a learning method that shares and transfers 
knowledge between different but related tasks or domains ( Cervi ˜no 
et al., 2021 ). The difference features of the bitemporal images are then 
derived for the change detection task. Moreover, we augment the 
modeling of TCs by quantifying the interactions of different feature 
layers within the extracted bitemporal semantic features, their corre -
sponding difference features, and within both by employing graph 
learning. This enhances the representation of changed areas and cate -
gories from a global perspective, thus improving SCD for complex 
scenarios. 
2.1. Feature extractor by Siamese multilevel perceptual aggregation

similar spectral and textural features. Here, we utilize a pyramid vision 
transformer, specifically PVT-V2 ( Fig. 2 a), pretrained on the ImageNet 
dataset ( Wang et al., 2022b ), as the backbone network of MPANet.

2.5. Change detection performance evaluation 
We compare our method with nine existing methods for BCD and 
SCD: BIT ( Chen et al., 2021a ), GAS-Net ( Zhang et al., 2023 ), HRSCD-3 
( Daudt et al., 2019 ), HRSCD-4 ( Daudt et al., 2019 ), FCCDN ( Chen 
et al., 2022a ), SSCDl ( Ding et al., 2022 ), Bi-SRNet ( Ding et al., 2022 ), 
PCFN ( Xia et al., 2022 ), and MTSCD-Net ( Cui and Jiang, 2023 ). The 
selection of these methods is based on the following criteria: 
BIT ( Chen et al., 2021a ) is a lightweight BCD model that combines 
CNN using a transformer to extract deep semantic features. It can also be 
applied to SCD. 
GAS-Net ( Zhang et al., 2023 ) is a state-of-the-art (SOTA) dual-branch 
model for BCD from high-resolution images. It integrates the correla -
tions between foreground and background areas to capture global-aware 
semantic cues, improving detection accuracy for various ground sce -
narios. GAS-Net can also be extended to SCD. 
HRSCD-3 ( Daudt et al., 2019 ) is a standard triple-branch network 
based on a multitask architecture for SCD, which introduces an extra 
change detection branch as a feature extractor to identify changed areas. 
It serves as a baseline for SCD comparison using multitask architectures. 
HRSCD-4 ( Daudt et al., 2019 ) improves semantic communication 
between change detection and segmentation tasks in HRSCD-3 by add -
ing an extra skip connection layer on Siamese CNNs. This is an advanced 
version of HRSCD-3 that is suitable for SCD comparison. 
FCCDN ( Chen et al., 2022a ) employs an encoder – decoder network 
with a triple-branch architecture based on feature constraint modules to 
enhance the detection capability of changed areas and categories. It 
achieved satisfactory results on various change detection datasets. 
SSCDl ( Ding et al., 2022 ) uses two Siamese CNN encoders as feature 
extractors, followed by a change detection network to identify changed 
areas. Compared to traditional triple-branch architectures ( Daudt et al., 
2019 ) that model different tasks individually, SSCDl learns more se -
mantic category information from bitemporal semantic features, 
improving SCD results. 
Bi-SRNet ( Ding et al., 2022 ) is a multitask convolutional network 
based on SSCDl. It considers TCs among bitemporal semantic features 
and serves as a SOTA method for SCD on the SECOND dataset. 
PCFN ( Xia et al., 2022 ) is a multitask model for SCD. It uses a Siamese 
classification network to extract multilevel semantic features and stacks 
these features for input into a soft fusion network, surpassing several 
traditional post-classification methods. 
MTSCD-Net ( Cui and Jiang, 2023 ) is a recent robust model based on 
multitask architecture for SCD. It enhances task correlations through a 
spatial enhancement module based on location priors, demonstrating 
superior detection results in various SCD scenarios.

3. Study datasets 
3.1. Public SCD datasets 
We first evaluate our method on two popular SCD datasets: SECOND 
( Yang et al., 2021 ) and HRSCD ( Daudt et al., 2019 ) datasets. The SEC -
OND dataset contains 4662 pairs of bi-temporal images with spatial 
resolutions ranging from 0.5 m to 3 m ( Yang et al., 2021 ), collected from 
various Chinese cities, including Chengdu, Shanghai, and Hangzhou. For 
the SECOND dataset, however, only 2968 pairs of bi-temporal images 
with ground-truth labels are available. These ground-truth labels have 
seven classes: one unchanged class and six land cover classes, corre -
sponding to ground, water, trees, low vegetation, buildings, and play -
grounds. These images are divided into smaller images with a size of 
256 × 256 pixels. We then randomly split these cropped images into 
training and testing datasets in an 8:2 ratio, generating 9500 image pairs 
for training and 2372 image pairs for testing. The HRSCD dataset con -
tains 291 pairs of bi-temporal aerial images with a size of 10000 ×
10000 pixels and spatial resolution of 0.5 m, collected from several cities 
and rural areas in France in 2006 and 2012 ( Daudt et al., 2019 ). It in -
cludes six classes: unchanged, forests, artificial surfaces, wetlands, 
agricultural areas, and water, with a majority of pixels labeled as un -
changed. The artificial surfaces and agricultural areas only account for 
0.6 % in this dataset ( Zhao et al., 2022 ). We first crop these images into 
256 × 256 pixels, and discard images with few changed pixels ( < 10 %). 
Next, we randomly split these images into training and testing datasets 
in an 8:2 ratio, generating 10,704 and 2675 image pairs for training and 
testing, respectively. 
3.2. Cropland SCD datasets 
We obtain two additional datasets from real applications to further 
evaluate the generalizability of our proposed method. These datasets are 
used to detect changes from cropland areas to non-cropland areas in 
Fuzhou and Xiamen cities, China, respectively. Most changed objects on 
the Fuzhou dataset with small sizes and changed scenarios are more 
complex than on the Xiamen dataset. For the Fuzhou dataset, we acquire 
three pairs of bi-temporal images from the Changle (CL) and Minhou 
(MH) districts, on the Google Earth platform with a spatial resolution of 
0.5 m ( Fig. 5 a). For the Xiamen dataset, two pairs of bi-temporal images 
are collected from the Google Earth platform in the Tongan (TA) and 
Xiangan (XA) districts ( Fig. 5 b). Detailed image information of these two 
datasets can be found in Table 1 . Both datasets include seven classes: 
unchanged, tree, cropland, building, water, bare land, and road, leading 
to six underlying “ from cropland areas to non-cropland areas ” cases. We 
first crop bi-temporal image pairs into smaller image pairs with a size of 
256 × 256 pixels, and then randomly split these cropped image pairs 
into training and testing datasets according to a 7:3 ratio. We finally 
obtain 6104 and 4542 image pairs for training and 2615 and 1946 image 
pairs for testing on the Fuzhou and Xiamen datasets, respectively.

3.3. Experiment details 
All experiments are implemented using a 24 GB NVIDIA GPU 
(GeForce RTX 3090). We use the Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) 
optimizer with initial learning rates of 0.005, 0.001, 0.001, and 0.001 
for model training on the SECOND, HRSCD, Fuzhou, and Xiamen data -
sets, respectively. We train our model for 80 epochs using a batch size of 
8 and a weight decay of 5 × 10
-4
. To train the nine existing models, we 
follow the recommended settings from previous studies. During training, 
we apply data augmentation techniques, such as random rotation and 
flipping, to all models.

We first conducted ablation studies on the SECOND dataset, incor -

overall score (OS) and F 1
scd 
values that were reduced by 4.91 % and 
6.06 %, respectively, compared to HGINet. This implies that the pro -

two semantic segmentation tasks and a change detection task within a 
multitask architecture to constrain the learning of semantic category 
information. We considered TCs among different semantic features to

5.1. Effectiveness of the proposed feature extractor 
Recent studies have indicated that CNN-based feature extractors are 
insufficient for modeling the long-distance contexts of ground objects, 
leading to poor detection results and low accuracy, particularly for 
complex objects ( Zheng et al., 2022 ). By integrating near- and long- 
distance contexts at multiple levels, MPANet enhanced the feature 
learning of large objects with complex shapes. This showed that HGINet, 
embedding MPANet, evidently exceeded other popular methods, such as 
based ResNet34, Efficient-B3, and PVT-V2. It obtained satisfactory 
detection results with well-delineated shapes, even for objects with 
similar spectral characteristics (see Fig. 7 ). Future efforts should focus 
on boundary regularization, e.g., by constructing auxiliary tasks to 
constrain the learning of geometric features, to further improve the 
geometric aspect of SCD ( Long et al., 2022 ; Li et al., 2023a ). 
5.2. TC module improves the identification of unchanged areas 
Conventional SCD with triple-branch architecture ( Daudt et al., 
2019; Yang et al., 2021 ) inadequately models the correlations between 
bitemporal semantic features. To address this issue, HGINet modeled the 
correlations using a lightweight TC module, enhancing the representa -
tion of bitemporal semantic features of unchanged areas. This can 
mitigate the effects of image differences caused by seasonal changes. 
Our TC module also performed better than two popular conventional 
cross-attention methods (see Fig. 9 ), namely CCA ( Huang et al., 2019 ) 
and Cot-SR ( Daudt et al., 2019 ), due to its capability in modeling the 
global correlations between bitemporal semantic features. Related 
studies have further indicated that integrating positional embedding can 
even improve the detection accuracy of SCD ( Chen et al., 2021b ), which 
is also an interesting direction for further study. 
5.3. SDI module improves the detection of complex scenarios 
Traditional CNNs face challenges in modeling the interactions be -
tween different layers of deep semantic features, particularly those 
learned in a long-distance context. Modeling such interactions within an 
SCD setting using bitemporal images is even more challenging. In 
contrast, GCNs excel in modeling feature interactions of nonstructured 
data and have been successfully applied in various computer vision tasks

5.4. Analysis of model complexity 
We evaluated the model complexity of the proposed HGINet and nine

we observed that using modules based on the attention mechanism (i.e., 
SFSR and TC modules) and GCN (i.e., SDI module) introduced fewer 
parameters and computation loads compared to MSCF based on tradi -
tional convolution operations ( Table 2 ). Our method has shown the 
highest agreement with the ground truth, however, an extremely

5.5. Analysis of model adaptability and transferability 
HGINet has been applied in extensive experiments using four SCD 
datasets collected from high-resolution remote sensing images. These 
datasets were particularly characterized by imbalanced categories. 
HGINet outperformed nine existing methods and proved suitable for 
SCD across diverse scenarios. Our results generally achieved higher 
detection accuracy in datasets with fewer types of changes, such as those 
for the areas of Fuzhou and Xiamen, compared to those with more 
complex changes, such as the SECOND and HRSCD datasets. Moreover, 
the detection accuracy in the Xiamen area, with fewer change scenarios, 
was higher than that for changed objects in the Fuzhou area, charac -
terized by small sizes and irregular distribution. Furthermore, our 
method exhibited high adaptability and successfully transferred it to 
different areas, even those with substantial seasonal and spectral dif -
ferences. However, challenges were identified in scenarios with vague 
boundaries and target objects surrounded by highly complex land 
covers. An issue was also discovered in the public SECOND and HRSCD 
datasets, which contained many incorrect ground truth labels ( Fig. 15 ), 
affecting model training and subsequent prediction. To address this 
issue, exploring the use of semisupervised or unsupervised learning 
techniques to generate high-quality pseudolabels could be beneficial for 
improving SCD results ( Sun et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2023 ). 
6. Conclusions 
In this study, we developed HGINet, a hierarchical semantic graph 
interaction network for SCD from bitemporal high-resolution remote 
sensing images. The experimental results allow us to conclude the 
following:  
1) HGINet demonstrated superior performance in detecting changed 
areas and categories, exceeding nine SOTA methods by 1.28 % – 
11.53 %, 1.78 % – 30.21 %, 10.32 % – 25.42 %, and 1.34 % – 17.69 % in 
F 1
scd 
measure on four SCD datasets. 
2) The proposed feature extractor (MPANet) efficiently extracted ob -
jects with well-delineated boundaries and shapes, making it well- 
suited for detecting object changes with diverse shapes and sizes. 
MPANet also outperformed other feature extractor networks such as 
ResNet34, Efficient-B3, and PVT-V2.  
3) Based on the cross-learning method, we designed a TC module to 
enhance the detection of unchanged areas by exploiting temporally 
correlated information, performing better than traditional cross- 
attention methods such as CotSR and CCA.  
4) We developed an SDI module using GCN to model the interactions of 
different feature layers in bitemporal semantic features, their cor -
responding difference features, and both. Incorporating such a 
module further improved the SCD results, leading to an increase of 
F 1
scd 
by 5.64 % and 4.08 % compared with the baseline method on 
the SECOND and HRSCD datasets, respectively.  
5) Transferring our method trained using one dataset (e.g., the Fuzhou 
dataset) to detect semantic changes in other areas (e.g., Xiamen 
areas) was successfully conducted, implying the high adaptability of 
HGINet. 
To further improve the detection performance of the proposed 
method, future works should investigate the effectiveness of auxiliary 
tasks (boundary), position information encoding, and semisupervised 
learning in constraining the learning of geometric features, enhancing 
the position representation of the changed areas, and producing high- 
quality pseudo labels, respectively.
